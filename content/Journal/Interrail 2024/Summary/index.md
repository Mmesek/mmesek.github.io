# Inspiration
In October 2022 I had an opportunity to travel alone for the first time somewhere I haven't been to before. While technically, I wasn't by myself, experience of stepping outside what I had known into the unknown on less than a week's notice, meeting people I would have only known digitally otherwise felt inspiring enough to considering traveling alone in the future within a realm of possibility.

Few years ago, maybe around March of 2022 or 2023, my sister once mentioned that she had a plan to go to Lisdoonvarna in Ireland for Matchmaking festival in September. At a time, it seemed to me like an ~~insane~~ interesting idea so I've researched a bit of possible ways to get there by land.

Why by land? There's Gamescom in Cologne, Germany in late August I already wanted to go for since 2019, however unforeseen obstacles prevented that from happening before. I also haven't really been to western Europe either. So it seemed like a great way to check Gamescom, see a bit of the world on the way and act as a wing-man for my sister. 

So there was the initial plan: Cologne -> Paris -> Dublin -> Lisdoonvarna.

Few months later I had a conversation with one of my friends where he outlined an idea to go around Europe with more people, maybe in a year or two so each of us could earn some money first. He wanted to avoid France and start from south to west: It was along the lines of Czech Republic -> Austria -> Italy -> Spain -> Portugal if I recall correctly.

In January 2023, the idea of becoming digital nomad seemed pretty on point to me, and I've told myself I would go through with my plan should I earn enough beforehand. Well, that didn't pan out, and I only went to Cologne. *(Meanwhile sister didn't go to Ireland as she seemed content with who she had at a moment)*

# Planning
In first half of 2024 however, finally managed to secure enough funds. *(I'm just sad of the price that had to be paid...)*. In May I've went to Italy with family and When In Rome, it came to my attention that there's a lot of discounts for youth below 24-26 years. As I was 24 at a moment it felt pretty much like now or never. Once I got back home, research on connections was done once again, mainly ferry prices.

And would you know it, there was a ferry to Ireland for less than 10€ (not including seating costs). Perfect to make it for the first week of September into Ireland too!

But if I would already go that far away from home, surely it's possible to go even further, right? Let's check out Portugal and why it seems to be a popular destination for Digital Nomads! As well as how Lisbon was one of safest cities. 
> *(Note: Research while writing this post conflicts with research done before doing the trip)*

When would I go though? June seemed too close after fantasy convention I was going to, and I didn't quite want to go in the middle of the summer due to potentially high temperatures in July. So that leaves August. From some reason, I also wanted to be back before birthday in September. Roughly a month trip, on which I thought I wouldn't spend more than 1k€. 

I also had two friends with whom I was meant to go to Gamescom with. We made some plans and free-cancelation reservations in April/May, but then in June I had some doubts regarding even going to there since I already been there year ago, and it didn't impress me at all *(Biggest games convention in Western Europe, a whole lot of halls full of people and just... meh?)* so I told one friend that I would only go for a day, maybe stay in Cologne for day more to go out with them. 

Meanwhile other friend I would go from Poland booked non-refundable plane tickets.
Well... enough to say that I had some meltdowns in regards to plans of this particular stage of trip and might have send conflicting messages to one friend.

## Initial Itinerary
I mentioned going to Portugal, but how can one get there from Ireland?

Either back through France but when going back there's need to go through Barcelona again when leaving Spain, or by the sea: there's ferry to Bilbao. Although it's... longer and more expensive than one to France. It also doesn't seem to be covered by Interrail's discount. 
But hey, it's an adventure! right? 

Then I need to come back home somehow, I've already been to Italy but heard Milan being often mentioned, also I didn't quite want to go through France again.

Okay, so new itinerary would look something like: Hamburg -> Cologne -> Amsterdam -> Paris -> Dublin -> Lisdoonvarna -> Cork -> Bilbao -> Porto -> Lisbon -> Madrid -> Barcelona -> Milan -> Prague.

Hmm... quite a bit of places, am I even gonna manage to make it in just 15 days of travel? How many days am I gonna spend in each place even? Day and Travel again? *(What If I just get continuous 21 travel days pass?)* How can I even get to Lisdoonvarna?

That last question was what made me realize that there isn't even a connection in Rail Planner app for this city. Okay, so let's cut it out *(besides, I doubt sister was even going to be interested in going there now)*. 

Even with updated plan, with ferries, I would go to Ireland for a week. That would require like 4 travel days at the very least. It also turned out that very cheap ferry to Ireland was now more expensive. Okay, let's cut it out.

I've freed a bunch of travel days, settling on 15 days in 2 months. Sadly it means in order to get to Portugal I have to go twice through Madrid and Barcelona. New itinerary?

Hamburg -> Cologne -> Amsterdam -> The Hague -> Rotterdam -> Ant-werp -> Paris -> Barcelona -> Madrid -> Porto -> Lisbon -> Madrid -> Valencia -> Barcelona -> Milan -> Prague -> Vienna.

I would go to cities between Amsterdam and Ant-werp on a single day so it would be well within 15 travel days. Still managed to get a bit of wiggle room if sheep hits the fan or if I wanted to exercise some chaos theory and just jump on trains at some point.

2 days before the trip I thought to myself, that I'm going to Hamburg. Denmark is not that far away. Copenhagen goes on the list right after Hamburg... when was I supposed to be in Cologne again? Friend mentioned he had tickets for week days. That's a little rough, as from some reason I wanted to be in Amsterdam, specifically on Friday/Saturday ~~*(and go out)*~~. 

In the end, I've stayed in Hamburg day longer, went for a day to Copenhagen and then straight to Amsterdam, even though the train I took was going to Cologne.

## Costs
One of more important, and probably most interesting graphs for people wanting to gauge how much such trip may cost, there you go, Western Europe's capitol cities in roughly...

![](expenses.png "HOW MUCH?!")

1314.53€! Phew, that... was a bit over the budget for sure. Add to that 410€ for Interrail ticket, and that results in 1724.53€.

On the day of my departure, the `EUR/PLN` pair was trading at average of `4.26`, so in total, *if* all my euro would be bought on the first day, at best rate, that would cost around `7565.75` PLN. On the day of my return 15th of September, it was `4.28` so it would cost `7600.24` PLN, just to give a totally scientific™ approximation of the currency drift throughout the journey. 

Realistic cost was... different. According to Wise/Revolut expenses summary, that would be `6142.53` and `1387.83`, for a total of `7530.36`. Still not exactly what I paid, as some buys were spread over weeks, but bit closer.

And what about **fees**? `0.27` in total, `0.09` per day, mainly to weekend when I was using Revolut or had to convert currency on the fly. Not as bad as I was worrying about, though... It was still avoidable!

# Improvements
## Aka things "wish I knew" before going:
- **[Famous Hostels](https://famoushostels.com)**: Up to -20% discount if you travel with Interrail or Flixbus. Of course, you are limited to specific hostels from the list, however I've found myself in some of them (as they were still the cheapest options at the moment) and thus, can confirm: they were some of better ones I've stayed at.
- **[Too Good To Go](https://toogoodtogo.com)**: Not every city is going to have big selection, but I was positively surprised by cost/food when I've tried it. Good if you don't want to spend too much on food AND you don't really care what you'll eat.
- **Avoid booking seat reservations at Barcelona or Madrid**: Spanish trains requires you to have seat reservation. Try to get all your reservations from Barcelona/Madrid *somewhere else*, otherwise waiting time is between 1h and 2h (It might even be worth paying extra 2€ fee by buying online via Interrail)
- **[Trek.Zone](https://trek.zone)**: Website with some useful tourism-related statistics about each country. Might be good for planning.
- **Throw TripAdvisor out of the window**: Seriously, some of worst recommendations; closed, expensive or just "international". There were some hidden gems there, sure, but that's rare. Very spammy too (80 emails in a week? I was *just* browsing for options...)
- **[PolarSteps](https://polarsteps.com)**: Travel Journal sort of app which contains spot recommendations. *(Which was recommended to me by a fellow traveler in Porto after she saw I was using Instagram for this purpose ;))*

## General tips
- **Compare multiple sources before booking (unless you have a discount code)**: This one is a bit tricky, some hostels are cheaper when you book online, some are cheaper at the reception, and rarely some are even cheaper at third party services like [Hostelworlds](https://hostelworld.com/) or [Trip](https://trip.com). I've even had really low price once when I've extended my stay at the reception (compared to online prices)
- **Phone with eSim**: Get a phone that supports *eSim*. This way you'll be able to purchase SIM with data plan for specific country. But it'll also let you go to countries where you can't use European Roaming like Switzerland or Monaco and save you worries.
- **Translator with Speech Recognition**: Make sure your phone doesn't have ducked up permissions and translator supports speech recognition. Speaking from experience.
- **Cash**: Carry some petty cash with you. Small bills like couple of 5€-10€. They are useful to pay deposit for keycard or towel at some hostels. (You'll get them back afterwards _if_ it was a deposit)
- **Travel card**: If you need to exchange money, you have *usually* two options: *slightly*-cheaper-than-bank-rates-on-week-days [Revolut](https://revolut.com/) and *conditionally*-no-fee-for-exchange [Wise](https://wise.com). Revolut on weekends is more expensive than rates from my bank due to their fees, however between Monday-Friday they are marginally (0.01 PLN) cheaper. Wise on the other hand is superior if: 
	- You have referral code that gives you -15GBP on fees (*cough* you can contact me if you need one *cough*)
	- You *transfer* money between currencies. Which means you'll need to receive different currency than the one you are sending. Discount does not apply on conversion between your accounts on the platform.
- **Think twice before using an ATM**: I've tried to use one in Prague. 4€ to withdraw equivalent of 20€. No thank you.
- **Check how cash-less country you plan to visit is**: Most places I've been to on this trip were *very* contact-less friendly. The only exception was Prague which apparently *loved* cash. I'm not entirely sure but feel like some places in Germany also had surcharge for using card.
- **Double check arrival/departure station**: Otherwise you might need to take public transportation to catch the train (like me from Paris Gare du Nord to Paris Gare Du Lyon). Also, check where the train goes after station where you are supposed to leave. I've had few cases when Rail Planner app told me to get off the train before reaching next station which was far closer to my destination or which would require me to walk a while while giving me an extra hour for a single transfer instead of two shorter ones. 

## What could be done better
- **Private VPN**: I've used free [ProtonVPN](https://protonvpn.com/), which did it's job very well. However there were situations when I couldn't use a website due to captchas. Though, I could've live without these websites anyway as they weren't even travel-related.
- **Better preparation**: what, when, in which order. Especially restaurants and food to try. A lot of time I was researching that stuff as I went, which worked. But I've ended up couple of times in places that were closed at the moment.
- Actually doing stuff aka **budget for activities**: go surfing, take a day off or do what you usually do abroad.
- Better **shoes** (perhaps even ones for off-road/hiking): non-water resistant ones are simply not cutting it. I've had water in mine in Amsterdam, and it was barely holding on by the end of the trip.
- **Bottle with filter**: in case tap water is not safe. Which was the case in Valencia. Also bigger bottle than 600ml could be useful.
- **Towel**: just so there's no need of relaying on accommodation rates, but also to go to the beach
- **Lock**: Technically optional if you don't carry anything valuable, but useful if you need peace of mind when you leave your stuff at a hostel.
- **Power-bank**: How many times did I go to the restaurant not because I was hungry but because I was running low on battery? Too many.
- Long **pants** (perhaps even convertible pants): Rain? No problem. Cold? No worries. Hot weather? Chill. Only shorts and going to windy or rainy places is... a choice for sure.
- Staying in each place longer: Ok, actually I didn't stay long on purpose, so that's entirely on me.
- Going to some meet-ups: Relaying on hostels is sadly a hit-or-miss
