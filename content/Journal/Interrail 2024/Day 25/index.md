To the train station! On my way I've passed couple just opening Boulangeries. When I've entered first that smelled good and seemed to have nice prices I was told that I need to pay at least 5€... Moving on, at the train station I've settled on pain aux raisins (far too expensive) that I thought from some reason would be still warm at 5am...

![](nice.jpg)

Train to my first connection at Cannes... Was late, so stayed on it until Nice were I managed to catch the train because.... It was also late

Passed through Monaco, Second breakfast from yesterday's crossaint (probably should've spend these 5€ earlier for day supply of them), a ticket check with yesterday's date and et voila, second connection to Genova! 

![](city.jpg)

At last, final connection to Milano, where I've been woken up by the conductor to check my ticket. All good.

Milan was a strange city: Italian mother calling her son to ask if his company is hiring; lad asking for money using google translate trying to get to my home town while constantly having a fake smile; an immigrant scammer giving me his freebie for free saying I'm not a racist and shopkeepers wanting my money, without offering Italian food quality... So what happened exactly?

![](milano_building.jpg)

![](milano_arch.jpg)

After reaching destination, I went to look for something to eat. I've found some nearby restaurant but prices kind of scared me away, so instead I looked at TooGoodToGo and there was a pizza place nearby which was pretty good if a bit cold. 

As I've tried to sit and enjoy my freshly bought (cold) Roman-pizza, a bee-like creature approached me. I was a hungry hobo and they wanted claim over my food? That only means one thing: Battle over Pizza! Loosing battle against windmills, if you asked Sancho Panza I suppose.

![](tgtg_pizza.jpg)

My brave, albeit clearly misguided efforts were recognized by another client who seemed to be looking for a small talk. Bee finally gave up, and stranger told me to ask them to reheat the pizza because cold "it's not good" (At least *some* recognize Italian quality!). 

Frankly, I haven't thought of asking before, so I went and did so. However, staff refused to heat it as what I bought was considered a takeout by them. Later, I've even offered to pay extra to which they just replied that "they don't do that here". Riiight...

Back to the stranger, we chatted a little more and eventually, she called her son asking if his company is not hiring by any chance. She left me with company name, told me I'll need Italian bank account, which I can open nearb (she actually pointed me in a direction of one across the street) and maybe learn a bit of Italian eventually too and I should be good to go. I honestly did not expect such turn of events, nor that it would happen right of the bat after leaving train station.

Few moments later as I went to look for something to drink, I've also met some dude that was asking people for money for return ticket to my home town. What a small world.

Took a stroll around a city and went to the park. There, interrail scared the sheep outta me by ghosting my pass while I was searching for connections out of Milano. 

![](IMG_20240912_180442368%201.jpg)

As I was walking, (possible) immigrant of African origin approached me with a bunch of colorful lines. When I was in Venice, Italy and later in Rome back in May, a similar strangers approached me, offering "handmade" *(made in China™ in bulk)* freebies, then starting talking about their families and that they need money. Well, a scheme seemed rather widespread so I was rather skeptical. I've replied that I don't have any cash (which was mostly true) however this one... just put one of these lines on my shoulder for me to take and said that it's free as I'm not racist. Also called me a good boy. What a day of unexpected encounters.
